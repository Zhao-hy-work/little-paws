Meet the animals

Seven different animal friends, seven different ways of sharing your home. Explore their personalities and the care they need.

🐱

Cats

A curious companion for someone who enjoys quiet company and short play sessions.

Before bringing one home: Daily play, clean litter trays, scratching and climbing spaces, and a quiet retreat. Cats need attention and long-term veterinary care.

🐶

Dogs

An active companion for someone who enjoys walks, training and sharing a daily routine.

Before bringing one home: Daily walks suited to age and health, reward-based training, company and a safe home. Arrange care when you are away; a garden does not replace walks.

🐰

Rabbits

Gentle explorers for someone who likes patient, floor-level interaction.

Before bringing one home: A suitable bonded rabbit companion, a large safe living and exercise area, plenty of hay, rabbit-proofing and rabbit-experienced veterinary care. Many dislike being picked up.

🐹

Hamsters

A small evening explorer for someone who likes quietly observing busy animals.

Before bringing one home: A large, well-designed enclosure, deep suitable bedding and an appropriately sized wheel. Let it sleep during the day. Syrian hamsters must live alone; wheel and digging sounds may disturb sleep.

🐹

Guinea pigs

Social little companions for someone who enjoys gentle interaction and a regular routine.

Before bringing one home: A compatible guinea-pig companion, a spacious enclosure and exercise area, hay, suitable vitamin-C-rich food and daily cleaning. They can be vocal and need gentle handling.

🐦

Budgies

Lively birds for someone who enjoys sound, patient training and social interaction.

Before bringing one home: Suitable bird companionship, a spacious flight cage and safe daily flight time, enrichment and an avian vet. Budgies are vocal; fumes, unsafe windows and other household hazards need attention.

🐠

Aquarium fish

An observation-based hobby for someone who enjoys calm routines and learning about habitats.

Before bringing one home: Choose a species first, then a suitably sized, cycled and filtered aquarium. Research temperature and social needs, test water and do regular maintenance. A small bowl is not a suitable shortcut.

Learn before choosing a pet

Small animals still need space, time and professional care. Check housing rules, allergies, costs and your plans for the animal's full lifespan.
