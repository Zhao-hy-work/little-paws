Animal guide

Rabbits

Gentle explorers for someone who likes patient, floor-level interaction.

Common types Feeding Home & daily care Health signs

01 / Meet the animal

Common types and appearance

Rabbits have long ears, strong hind legs and continuously growing teeth. Their coat and ear shape vary, but all need space to hop, stretch and hide.

Examples you may meet

  • Mixed-breed rabbits: Common in rescues; size, ear position and coat colour vary.
  • Lop-eared rabbits: Ears hang down; ear health needs particular attention.
  • Rex rabbits: Known for a short, plush-feeling coat; still need the same space, food and company as other rabbits.

These are examples, not a ranking of the best pets. Individuals can differ from typical descriptions.

02 / Food and water

What and how to feed

  • Good-quality hay or grass should always be available and form the main part of the diet. It supports the teeth and digestion.
  • Offer suitable leafy greens daily and a measured amount of plain rabbit pellets. Introduce new greens slowly.
  • Provide fresh water at all times. Avoid muesli-style mixes and sugary treats; ask a rabbit-savvy vet about the right amounts for your rabbit.

03 / Everyday life

A suitable home and daily care

  • Plan for a compatible bonded rabbit companion and a large, secure area for living and exercise.
  • Provide hiding places, non-slip flooring, digging and chewing opportunities, and protection from heat and predators.
  • Rabbit-proof cables and unsafe plants. Many rabbits dislike being picked up; interact at floor level.

04 / Keep watching

Health and further reading

A rabbit that stops eating or producing droppings needs urgent veterinary advice.

For more detailed care requirements, start with RSPCA rabbit care RSPCA rabbit diet. Check species-specific needs with a qualified vet or rescue before bringing an animal home.
