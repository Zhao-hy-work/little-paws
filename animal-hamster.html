Animal guide

Hamsters

A small evening explorer for someone who likes quietly observing busy animals.

Common types Feeding Home & daily care Health signs

01 / Meet the animal

Common types and appearance

Hamsters have short tails, small rounded ears and cheek pouches for carrying food. They are usually most active in the evening and at night.

Examples you may meet

  • Syrian hamster: Larger hamster with a rounded body; adults must live alone.
  • Roborovski hamster: Very small and quick, often sandy coloured with pale markings above the eyes.
  • Dwarf hamster: A small group including Campbell’s and Winter White hamsters; exact species matters for care and handling.

These are examples, not a ranking of the best pets. Individuals can differ from typical descriptions.

02 / Food and water

What and how to feed

  • Offer a balanced hamster food appropriate to the species, following the label for a starting portion.
  • Scatter some food through clean bedding so the hamster can forage. Offer small amounts of suitable vegetables; introduce new foods gradually.
  • Provide fresh water every day. Remove fresh food before it spoils, but do not disturb all stored dry food at once. Avoid sugary treats and sticky food.

03 / Everyday life

A suitable home and daily care

  • Choose a large, well-ventilated enclosure with deep, safe bedding for burrowing and a solid wheel sized for the hamster’s back to stay straight.
  • Provide hides and safe chew items. Keep the enclosure away from heat, loud noise and direct sunlight.
  • Let a sleeping hamster rest during the day. Check the exact species before considering any social housing; Syrian hamsters live alone.

04 / Keep watching

Health and further reading

Check appetite, activity and breathing. Seek advice from a small-animal vet if the hamster stops eating, has diarrhoea or appears injured.

For more detailed care requirements, start with RSPCA pet care guides. Check species-specific needs with a qualified vet or rescue before bringing an animal home.
