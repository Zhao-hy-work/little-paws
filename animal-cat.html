Animal guide

Cats

A curious companion for someone who enjoys quiet company and short play sessions.

Common types Feeding Home & daily care Health signs

01 / Meet the animal

Common types and appearance

Cats have flexible bodies, whiskers, retractable claws and coats in many colours. Coat length and colour do not predict an individual cat’s personality.

Examples you may meet

  • Domestic shorthair: Mixed ancestry; short coat in many colours and patterns. Often easier to groom than long-haired cats.
  • Domestic longhair: Mixed ancestry with a longer coat; needs regular brushing to prevent tangles.
  • Siamese: Usually a pale body with darker face, ears, paws and tail; often vocal and people-oriented.

These are examples, not a ranking of the best pets. Individuals can differ from typical descriptions.

02 / Food and water

What and how to feed

  • Choose a complete food labelled for the cat’s life stage; kittens and adult cats have different needs.
  • Measure portions using the food guide and your vet’s advice. Offer clean water at all times; some cats drink more readily from several bowls or a fountain.
  • Offer treats sparingly. Avoid feeding human foods that may be harmful, including onions and chocolate. Ask a vet before changing a medical diet.

03 / Everyday life

A suitable home and daily care

  • Provide a clean litter tray in a quiet place, scratching posts, safe climbing places and somewhere to hide.
  • Play every day with safe toys, then allow the cat to rest. Give it a choice about being touched or picked up.
  • Keep up with vaccinations, parasite prevention and vet checks as advised locally.

04 / Keep watching

Health and further reading

A cat that stops eating, struggles to urinate or changes behaviour suddenly needs prompt veterinary advice.

For more detailed care requirements, start with RSPCA cat care. Check species-specific needs with a qualified vet or rescue before bringing an animal home.
