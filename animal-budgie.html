Animal guide

Budgies

Lively birds for someone who enjoys sound, patient training and social interaction.

Common types Feeding Home & daily care Health signs

01 / Meet the animal

Common types and appearance

Budgies are small parrots with a curved beak, long pointed tail and striped markings on the head or wings. Feather colour varies widely.

Examples you may meet

  • Australian or pet-type budgie: Usually smaller and slimmer; green and yellow are common, but many colour varieties exist.
  • English or show-type budgie: Often larger with a fuller head and longer feathers.
  • Colour varieties: Blue, white, yellow and mixed-colour birds are common; colour does not change basic care needs.

These are examples, not a ranking of the best pets. Individuals can differ from typical descriptions.

02 / Food and water

What and how to feed

  • Offer a balanced budgie diet and suitable fresh vegetables. Ask an avian vet about a gradual transition if the bird currently eats only seed.
  • Replace water daily and wash food dishes. Remove uneaten fresh food before it spoils.
  • Seeds and millet can be useful as treats or training rewards, but a seed-only diet is not balanced. Avoid avocado, chocolate and alcohol.

03 / Everyday life

A suitable home and daily care

  • Provide a spacious flight cage with varied perches, safe toys and room for supervised daily flight in a bird-safe room.
  • Budgies are social. Research suitable bird companionship and allow gentle, patient interaction without forcing handling.
  • Keep birds away from fumes, aerosols, non-stick cookware fumes, open windows and other hazards. Find an avian vet in advance.

04 / Keep watching

Health and further reading

Birds can hide illness. Fluffed feathers, sitting low, breathing changes or reduced eating call for prompt avian-vet advice.

For more detailed care requirements, start with RSPCA pet care guides. Check species-specific needs with a qualified vet or rescue before bringing an animal home.
